Train speeds — rated / MCS / trip

Couplings (API 671)

Assumptions. Motor slip is a flat 1% for induction machines; steam expansion uses the Peng-Robinson EOS for H₂O with saturation-line correlations (±3% class on isentropic drop); turbine efficiency is a size correlation, 60% at 1 MW rising to 80% at 30 MW; gas turbine site derating uses the barometric altitude factor, −0.6%/°C above the 15 °C ISO day, a humidity air-density correction and 2% duct losses. MCS and trip follow API 612 practice: 105% and 110% × MCS. Coupling factors are API 671 screening values — motor short-circuit transients are a torsional (API 684) question, not sized here.

Gearbox rating and basic design

Single-mesh, double-helical, parallel-shaft speed increaser: bull gear on the driver shaft, pinion on the compressor shaft. The pinion is sized from the API 613 tooth pitting index (K-factor) at service factor × rated torque; tooth counts are then snapped to a hunting combination and the compressor is re-solved at the exact output speed they give.

Assumptions. Screening sizing only — a vendor rating to AGMA 6011 / ISO 6336 (bending, scuffing, thermal) will differ. Service factors are the customary API 613 minimums per driver class (API 677 floor 1.1 for general purpose); K-factor allowables are typical for through-hardened vs carburised-and-ground teeth; face width F = 1.0 × pinion diameter net of the apex gap; helix angle 30°, thrust cancelling by symmetry. Mesh loss 1.5% + 0.5% bearings — the same 2% the driver-rating chain has always carried.

Lube oil system (API 614)

Oil flows from the train's heat losses at the design temperature rise, then the console: pumps, reservoir, cooler, filter, accumulator — and the rundown (overhead) tank sized from the coast-down time below.

Across a bearing, supply to drain. 15–25 K is usual.
API 614 special purpose works from 8 minutes of normal flow.

Coast-down (rundown) curve

J·dω/dt = −(T₀ + c₁ω + c₂ω²) on the combined driver + gearbox + compressor inertia, reflected to the driver shaft. The retarding torque is anchored to the train's mechanical losses at rated speed, split 20% boundary friction / 50% viscous / 30% windage. Process-gas braking is excluded on purpose: it shortens the coast-down, so ignoring it is conservative for the rundown tank.

Assumptions. ISO VG 32/46 oil (ρ 860 kg/m³, cp 1.95 kJ/kg·K). Reservoir: retention + 5 min charge + 15% freeboard, per API 614 special-purpose custom. Pumps: main at 120% of normal flow, aux a 100% spare. Accumulator bridges a 6 s aux-pump start. Rundown tank: bearing flow × coast-down time × 1.2, elevated for 0.7 barg at the bearings during rundown + 20% line loss. Inertias are ±50% correlations unless overridden with vendor numbers.

Dry gas seal system (API 692)

Seal sizing pressure is the settle-out pressure (or suction, whichever governs), computed from the suction and discharge system volumes on the Process Data tab. Leakage and supply flows are screening correlations for flare-load and N₂-demand estimates — the seal OEM's curve is the contractual number.

Factory acceptance testing (API 692)

Assumptions. Tandem arrangement by default, tandem with intermediate labyrinth when H₂S is present. Primary leakage ∝ seal size × sealing pressure, anchored at ~3 Nm³/h for a 75 mm seal at 100 bara — real seals vary ±50% with gas, temperature and face design. Seal gas supply holds ≥5 m/s across the inner process labyrinth; separation gas is a fixed small N₂ flow per seal. Test criteria and sequence follow customary API 692 Part 3 FAT practice; the purchased edition and the approved vendor procedure define the binding hold times and acceptance values.

Case management

Datasheet

Assumptions and limitations

Purpose. This is an estimation and screening tool for preliminary sizing and for checking vendor proposals. It is not a substitute for a vendor selection, an API 617 rotordynamic analysis, or a rigorous process simulation.

Impeller eye geometry. The inlet relative Mach number needs an eye diameter and hub ratio that are really the vendor's to choose. A correlation of eye-to-tip ratio against flow coefficient with a fixed 0.30 hub ratio is used instead. This is the largest single simplification in the aerodynamic model.

Efficiency is correlated, not calculated. Polytropic efficiency comes from an inlet-flow correlation with a flow-coefficient derate. A real stage varies by several points with the vendor's diffuser and return-channel design.

Equal head split, constant diameter. Head is split equally between the impellers of a section and the tip diameter is held constant, so the flow coefficient falls stage by stage as the gas densifies. A wide φ spread across a section is the signal that intercooling or a second body is needed.

Frame rules are simplified. They encode common practice and the API 617 hydrogen partial-pressure rule for barrel casings. Vendor frame ranges differ substantially.

Speed and stage overrides skip the auto-adjustment loop. With a section left on Auto, the stage count grows until every limit is met. Setting it manually fixes the count exactly as entered — the limits are still checked and flagged, but the tool will not silently add stages to make an out-of-limits case look clean.

Integrally geared pinion pairing. Two impellers sharing a pinion are forced to run at one common speed — the geometric mean of what each would want on its own — so at most one of them sits at its true aerodynamic optimum. That compromise is real and is shown in the reported flow coefficients, not hidden. Gear ratios and pinion counts are screening rules, not vendor gearbox data.

No phase equilibrium. Condensation at an intercooler is flagged from a Wilson K-value dew-point estimate only. No flash is performed and the composition is held constant through the machine — a real knockout drum would change it.

Equation of state accuracy. Peng-Robinson and SRK are reliable for hydrocarbons away from the critical point. Near the critical point, and for light gases such as hydrogen, helium and nitrogen well above their critical temperature at high pressure, all three models lose accuracy — SRK and Lee-Kesler usually do better than PR there. Compare models before trusting a number.

Rotordynamics. The bearing span, critical speed and cross-coupling figures are order-of-magnitude screening indicators only.

Train accessories are screening estimates. The gearbox sizing (API 613 K-factor with customary service factors), driver models (steam TSR from a PR-EOS expansion, gas turbine site derates, motor pole selection), lube oil console (API 614 special-purpose customs), coast-down (correlated inertias, 20/50/30 loss split) and dry gas seal flows (size × pressure leakage correlation) are all requisition-level numbers. Vendor rating calculations, the governing API editions and the purchaser's specification take precedence; inertia overrides on the Lube Oil tab accept vendor rotor data as it arrives.

1. Pressure reference (gauge / absolute)

Only the suction pressure P₁ and each section's discharge pressure P₂ are "a pressure relative to atmosphere" — an intercooler Δp is already a difference and this setting doesn't touch it. Everywhere else in the engine, pressure is absolute Pa always; gauge/absolute is purely how the number is typed in and displayed.

P_abs = P_gauge + P_atmospheric P_gauge = P_abs − P_atmospheric

Changing the atmospheric pressure reference re-derives the display for values already entered (the same way switching SI ⇄ US units re-displays a value without changing what it physically means) — it does not silently move your process case. The suction field reads its typed value fresh on every calculation, so editing the atmospheric reference updates it immediately; each section's discharge pressure is stored once as absolute and re-displayed, so it stays put until you retype it.

2. Gas mixture properties

Shown on the Gas Composition tab, computed once the composition is normalised to mole fractions yᵢ.

MW = Σ yᵢ · MWᵢ molecular weight Tc = Σ yᵢ · Tcᵢ (Kay's rule) pseudo-critical temperature Pc = Σ yᵢ · Pcᵢ (Kay's rule) pseudo-critical pressure ω = Σ yᵢ · ωᵢ (Kay's rule) pseudo acentric factor Rsp = R / (MW/1000) specific gas constant, J/(kg·K)

Kay's rule (a plain mole-fraction average of the pure-component critical properties) is what feeds the Lee-Kesler model and the reduced-property warning on that tab. Peng-Robinson and SRK don't use a single pseudo-critical point — they mix at the level of the EOS's own a and b parameters, described next.

3. Equation of state — compressibility Z

Peng-Robinson and SRK are the same cubic equation with different constants, so one derivation covers both. Selectable on the Machine tab; drives every density, enthalpy and entropy value in the tool.

P = RT/(v−b) − aα(T) / [(v+δ₁b)(v+δ₂b)] δ₁ δ₂ Ωa Ωb m(ω) SRK: 1 0 0.42748 0.08664 0.480 + 1.574ω − 0.176ω² PR: 1+√2 1−√2 0.45724 0.07780 0.37464 + 1.54226ω − 0.26992ω² (1978 heavy-fluid form above ω=0.49: 0.379642 + 1.48503ω − 0.164423ω² + 0.016666ω³) aᵢ = Ωa·R²·Tcᵢ²/Pcᵢ bᵢ = Ωb·R·Tcᵢ/Pcᵢ α = [1 + m(1 − √(T/Tcᵢ))]² van der Waals mixing: a_m = ΣΣ yᵢyⱼ(1−kᵢⱼ)√(aᵢαᵢ · aⱼαⱼ) b_m = Σ yᵢ bᵢ A = a_m·P/(R²T²) B = b_m·P/(RT) Cubic in Z: SRK: Z³ − Z² + (A−B−B²)Z − AB = 0 PR: Z³ − (1−B)Z² + (A−3B²−2B)Z − (AB−B²−B³) = 0 Solved via the trigonometric/Cardano method (no initial guess, cannot diverge); Z is the largest real root with Z > B (the vapour root).

Binary interaction parameters kᵢⱼ are a sparse table (N₂, CO₂, H₂S and H₂ pairs with the hydrocarbons; everything else defaults to 0), reused unchanged between PR and SRK, which is normal practice for screening work.

Enthalpy and entropy departure — one general form for both models, parameterised by δ₁/δ₂:

H − H^ig = RT(Z−1) + (T·da_m/dT − a_m)/(b_m(δ₁−δ₂)) · ln[(Z+δ₁B)/(Z+δ₂B)] S − S^ig = R·ln(Z−B) + (da_m/dT)/(b_m(δ₁−δ₂)) · ln[(Z+δ₁B)/(Z+δ₂B)]

Lee-Kesler is a different approach: three-parameter corresponding states, using Kay's-rule pseudo-critical Tc/Pc/ω (above) rather than a mixture EOS. Two reference fluids — "simple" (ω=0) and "reference" (n-octane, ω=0.3978) — are each solved from the same modified BWR form for their own Z and departures, then blended:

Z = Z⁽⁰⁾ + (ω/ω_ref)·(Z⁽ʳ⁾ − Z⁽⁰⁾) H−H^ig = H⁽⁰⁾ + (ω/ω_ref)·(H⁽ʳ⁾ − H⁽⁰⁾) (same blend for S−S^ig)

Reliable for non-polar mixtures of similar-sized molecules; the tool warns if you pick it for a mixture containing water, methanol or ammonia.

4. Real-gas Cp, Cv, isentropic exponent, speed of sound

Shown on the Gas Composition tab as "Real Cp", "Cp / Cv", "Isentropic volume exponent" and "Acoustic velocity".

Cp = (∂H/∂T)_P central difference of H(T,P), ΔT ≈ max(0.02 K, T·10⁻⁵) Cp − Cv = −T·(∂v/∂T)²_P / (∂v/∂P)_T triple-product relation kᵥ = (Cp/Cv) · (−v/P) / (∂v/∂P)_T isentropic volume exponent, kᵥ a = √(kᵥ · P · v / MW) acoustic velocity, a₁

All three derivatives come from numerically differencing v(T,P), which keeps Peng-Robinson, SRK and Lee-Kesler on exactly the same footing — Lee-Kesler only ever gives Z(T,P), not a P(v,T) form to differentiate analytically, so this is the one method that works for all three.

5. Compression path — polytropic head, three ways

The integrated path is what the tool actually sizes the machine on. Schultz and the simple single-step form are shown alongside on the Results tab as a cross-check — divergence between the three is itself the sign the gas is far from ideal.

Integrated (used for sizing): dHp = v·dP polytropic head increment dh = v·dP / ηp small-stage definition of polytropic efficiency → stepped in ~40 sub-stages; T₂ falls out of the real enthalpy balance, not an ideal-gas exponent shortcut. Schultz (cross-check): n = ln(P₂/P₁) / ln(v₁/v₂) nₛ = ln(P₂/P₁) / ln(v₁/v₂ₛ) (v₂ₛ at the isentropic endpoint) f = (h₂ₛ−h₁) / [(nₛ/(nₛ−1))·(P₂v₂ₛ − P₁v₁)] Schultz correction factor Hp = f · (n/(n−1)) · (P₂v₂ − P₁v₁) Simple single-step polytropic (cross-check): n = 1 / (1 − (k−1)/(k·ηp)) Hp = (n/(n−1)) · Z₁ · Rsp · T₁ · (rp^((n−1)/n) − 1)

A note on that last line: the coefficient is n/(n−1), not n/exp with exp=(n−1)/n — those aren't the same thing, and writing it the second way overstates the head by a factor of n. That's the exact bug this tool's cross-check caught in the hub's older CompressorCalc_9 estimator; the three methods here agree to within a percent or two on any duty that isn't strongly non-ideal, which is the whole point of showing all three.

6. Polytropic efficiency prediction

Shown as "Polytropic efficiency" on the Results tab; overridable on the Machine tab.

Qcfm = 0.5885 · Q₁ [m³/h] η_base = clamp( 0.61 + 0.03·log₁₀(max(Qcfm,1)), 0.70, 0.85 ) φ-penalty (parabolic derate about the best-efficiency point φ=0.075): d = (φ − 0.075) / 0.075 f = clamp( 1 − 0.16·d² , 0.80, 1.0 ) ηp = clamp( η_base · f , 0.68, 0.87 )

The flow-only base correlation is the same one the hub's earlier CompressorCalc_9 tool uses, so the two agree on the same duty; the φ-penalty is layered on top once a flow coefficient is known, and the whole thing re-solves iteratively since φ itself depends on the stage geometry that depends on ηp.

7. Stage aerodynamics

These are exactly the columns in the "Stage by stage" table. All consistent SI (Hp in J/kg, U₂ in m/s, D₂ in m, N in rpm, Q₁ in m³/s).

ψ = Hp_stage / U₂² head (work) coefficient φ = Q₁ / (¼π·D₂²·U₂) flow coefficient U₂ = π·D₂·N / 60 tip speed Mu₂ = U₂ / a₁ peripheral Mach number Sizing solve, for a target ψ and φ: U₂ = √(Hp_stage / ψ) D₂ = √(4·Q₁ / (π·φ·U₂)) N = 60·U₂ / (π·D₂) Given a fixed N instead (manual speed, or a pinion's shared speed): U₂ = √(Hp_stage / ψ) D₂ = 60·U₂/(π·N) φ = Q₁ / (¼π·D₂²·U₂)

Inlet relative Mach (M rel) needs an eye diameter and hub ratio that are really the vendor's to choose — the largest single simplification in this aerodynamic model:

eyeRatio = clamp( 0.40 + 1.9·φ^0.55 , 0.35, 0.72 ) D₁/D₂ correlated to φ D₁ = eyeRatio · D₂ eye diameter hubRatio = 0.30 fixed assumption A_eye = ¼π·D₁²·(1 − hubRatio²) Cx1 = Q₁ / A_eye U1 = U₂·eyeRatio W1 = √(Cx1² + U1²) M rel = W1 / a₁

8. Stage count & speed solve

How "Number of stages" and "Predicted speed" on the Results tab are actually chosen.

Allowable tip speed = min( impeller mechanical limit, Mu₂max · a₁ ) ψ = min( target ψ, impeller's own ψ max ) Head per stage max = ψ · (allowable tip speed)² Seed stage count = ceil( total polytropic head / head-per-stage max ) → march the section at that count, check every stage's Mu₂, M rel, U₂, φ against their limits; if any is exceeded, add a stage and re-march (up to 20 stages). Left on Auto, this loop is what decides the count. Manual override: the requested count is used exactly - the loop above is skipped, not grown to "fix" a limit violation. Limits are still checked and flagged; a manual count just isn't silently overruled.

Speed and diameter, once the stage count is set:

speedLimited — the free (auto) solve wanted a speed above the architecture's cap; pinned at the cap sizeLimited — the free solve wanted a diameter below the minimum; pinned at the minimum overSpeed — the FINAL speed (auto or manual) is still above the cap - flagged, not hidden underSize — the FINAL diameter (auto or manual) is still below the minimum - flagged, not hidden

A manual speed is put through the exact same overSpeed/underSize check as the automatic one — there's no separate "trust the user" code path, so a hand-typed speed that would build an impossible impeller shows the same warning an automatic one would.

9. Integrally geared: pinion pairing & gear ratio

Only applies when the architecture is set to Integrally geared. Each section is one impeller; pinions carry one or two of them.

Auto-pairing: consecutive stages pair up (1&2, 3&4, ...). With an odd stage count, the leftover single-impeller pinion goes to whichever stage's free-solve speed differs most from its neighbour's - the pairing that would have cost the most aerodynamically is the one skipped. Common pinion speed - the geometric mean of the members' free speeds, so the compromise is split evenly in log space rather than favouring the faster stage: N_pinion = (N₁ · N₂ · ... )^(1/count) then clamped to maxSpeed and minD2 Gear ratio: ratio = N_pinion / N_bullGear

Each impeller on a shared pinion is then resized to that common speed via the "fixed N" sizing solve above (§7) — so a paired stage runs off its own aerodynamic optimum, and that cost shows up directly as its φ moving away from the 0.055–0.095 best-efficiency band, not hidden anywhere. A single mesh above a ~20:1 ratio, or more than 4 pinions on one bull gear, gets flagged as needing an intermediate gear stage or a split into two machines.

10. Compressor type & casing selection

The architecture (beam-type / integrally geared / overhung) is your choice on the Machine tab — this only decides the casing within it, and whether the duty actually suits the architecture picked.

Beam-type casing: H₂ partial pressure at discharge ≥ 14 bar (200 psi) → vertically split barrel (API 617) else discharge pressure ≥ 60 bara → vertically split barrel else discharge ≥ 40 bara AND MW < 12 → vertically split barrel else → horizontally split Suitability warnings (any architecture): IGC with H₂ partial pressure ≥ 14 bar, or discharge ≥ 60 bara, or MW < 12 → a beam-type barrel is flagged as the safer choice Overhung needing more than 1 stage, or discharge ≥ 20 bara → flagged as needing a different architecture Q₁ > 200,000 m³/h with ≤ 2 stages → axial machine may suit better > 9 stages in one beam-type body → tandem or two-body train suggested Predicted speed above the architecture's usual ceiling → geared drive implied Predicted speed < 3000 rpm → direct low-speed motor drive suggested

11. Power & driver rating

Gas power = ṁ · Hp_total / ηp W Shaft power = Gas power / η_mech η_mech default 0.985 Gear loss: integrally geared: 1.5% per pinion mesh (charged per pinion, not one flat figure) beam/overhung with a gearbox: flat 2% Coupling loss: 0.5% (always) Power at driver = Shaft power · (1 + gear loss) · (1 + coupling loss) Driver rating = Power at driver · (1 + margin) margin default 10% (API 617: 110% of max power) Standard size: next value ≥ rating from the IEC kW or NEMA hp table.

12. Rotordynamic screening (indicative only)

Beam-type and overhung only — this beam-rotor model describes a shaft between two bearings and doesn't apply to an integrally geared pinion, which is short, stiff and overhung; the pinion table's gear ratio and tip speed are the relevant figures there instead.

pitch = 0.33 · D₂ impeller pitch, correlated to tip diameter bearing span = pitch · stages + 1.6·D₂ shaft diameter = 0.30 · D₂ L/D = span / shaft diameter First critical of a bare simply-supported steel shaft (sqrt(E/ρ) = 5135 m/s for steel; a 0.55 knockdown for impeller/collar mass): N_critical ≈ 121000 · 0.55 · d / L² rpm, order of magnitude flexibility ratio = operating speed / N_critical Aero cross-coupling indicator (API 617 Level-I style): Qa = (shaft power[kW] / speed) · stages · (ρ_discharge / ρ_suction)

Flags: L/D > 12 (flexible rotor), flexibility ratio > 2.5 (runs well above the estimated first critical), Qa > 8 (high power/stage-count/density-rise combination — consider a shunt-injected or hole-pattern seal). All of this is order-of-magnitude screening, not a substitute for an API 617 Level I/II lateral and stability analysis.

13. Dew-point screening

Checked at suction and at every intercooler outlet; warns rather than blocks the calculation.

Wilson K-value, per component: Kᵢ = (Pcᵢ/P) · exp[ 5.373·(1+ωᵢ)·(1 − Tcᵢ/T) ] dewSum = Σ yᵢ/Kᵢ condensing if dewSum > 1

A screening estimate only — no flash is performed and the composition is never changed through the machine. If the suction condition itself is well past its dew point (dewSum > 1.5) the calculation stops outright, since a centrifugal compressor needs dry vapour at inlet; a milder exceedance, or one at an intercooler, is flagged as a warning instead.

14. Where the default limits come from

All editable on the Machine tab; these are the shipped defaults.

ψ target 0.52 (usable 0.42–0.65) φ usable 0.010–0.150, best-efficiency 0.055–0.095 Mu₂ max — by impeller construction, not one fixed number for every stage: cast/high-MW 1.00 closed steel, standard 1.05 closed steel, HS 1.10 open/semi-open (IGC) 1.30 titanium 1.35 M rel max 0.85 Max discharge temperature 150 °C (material/seal/polymerisation limit) Max stages per beam-type body ~9 Mechanical efficiency 0.985 Tip-speed limit by construction (m/s): cast 260 · standard steel 320 · high-strength steel 375 · open 500 · titanium 600 Max shaft speed: 20,000 rpm (beam/overhung, closed impellers) · 60,000 rpm (integrally geared pinion) Min impeller diameter: 180 mm (closed) · 100 mm (open) — room for shaft, seals and bearings Integrally geared: max 4 pinions, max 2 impellers/pinion, max gear ratio ~20:1, ~1.5% loss per mesh

One entry here isn't a solver limit at all — a screening check against real fleet experience, shown on the Charts tab and in the Results warnings. It doesn't feed the solve and never clamps anything; a result outside it is worth a second look, not necessarily wrong.

Average head per stage = Hp_section / nStages Fleet band, closed impellers: 19,429 – 41,847 J/kg (6,500 – 14,000 ft·lbf/lbm) from a survey of 60+ actual compressor sections across multiple equipment suppliers open/semi-open impellers legitimately run above this band — the survey covers closed impellers only Tip speed, same survey, closed impellers: 198.1 – 274.3 m/s (650 – 900 ft/sec) checked per stage, not per section — every impeller has its own U₂

Two published Aungier correlations are also carried, both plotted on the Charts tab. The efficiency one can drive the calculation if you select it on the Machine tab; the length ratio is reference only.

Aungier polytropic efficiency = f(φ) for a vaned or vaneless diffuser digitised from the published characteristic and linearly interpolated (good to ~±0.01, not an algebraic restatement of Aungier's own equations) depends on φ alone — no machine-size term, which is why very low φ falls away so steeply Aungier impeller axial length: L/D₂ = 0.014 + 0.023·(D₂/D₁) + 1.58·φ the published figure fixes the eye ratio and draws one straight line; here D₁/D₂ comes from the stage actually solved, so it tracks real geometry

15. Factory acceptance test (FAT)

Independent recomputation of a vendor shop-test report from its own raw readings, plus a line-by-line cross-check against what the vendor stated. Source: E. Wilcox (Chevron Energy Technology Company), Performance testing guidelines for centrifugal compressors, Hydrocarbon Processing, August 2007, pp. 59-69 — cited as [W] — layered on ASME PTC 10 and API 617. Equation numbers below are the paper's.

The vendor-report back-solve ([W] Fig. 6) — the heart of this tab A report always states discharge pressure and shaft (or gas) power; it does not always state discharge temperature. Guess T2, compute head/efficiency/gas power at that T2, and bisect T2 until the computed gas power matches the target: 1. hp_gas = hp_shaft - hp_mech approximate mechanical losses first 2. guess T2 3. compute Hp, eta_P and hp_gas at that T2 from the EOS 4. iterate T2 until hp_gas matches the target 5. read off Phi, mu_P and eta_P Works even when the report gives no discharge temperature at all. Non-dimensional form ([W] Eqs. 12-15) — note the convention (12) mu_P = (Hp / nImpellers) / u_tip^2 PER IMPELLER, NO factor of 2 (13) eta_P = Hp / (h2 - h1) (14) Phi = Q / (N D^3), or the practical Q/N (15) M = pi.D.N / sqrt(k1.Z1.R.T1) mu_P is the convention OEM curves are usually drawn in. The SAT tab's psi = 2H/u_tip^2 is RELATED but not equal: psi = 2.mu_P. Both appear side by side so the two tabs stay reconcilable on the same machine rather than looking like they disagree by a factor of two. Flow-meter correction for gas composition ([W] Eqs. 1-4) (1) Q = K.sqrt(dP) K = f(P,T,Mw) - a DCS meter factor (2) Q_A = Q.sqrt(P_A.T_D.Z_D.Mw_D / (P_D.T_A.Z_A.Mw_A)) standard volume A DCS flowmeter is rarely compensated for molecular weight, since that needs an online gas analyser. [W] Table 1: reading MW as 34 instead of 48 moved 27.4 MMscfd to 32.5 and 5,775 hp to 7,036 hp - a 22% power error from a sampling-temperature mistake alone. Losses (8) hp_MECH = mdot.cp.dT_oil / 33,000 lube-oil heat balance, [W]'s own US units Table 3: mechanical loss as a % of GAS power, banded by gas power (3.0% below 3,000 hp, down to 1.5% above 10,000 hp). Table 4: gearbox efficiency by type (helical 97-99%, herringbone ~96-99%, straight/spiral bevel 95-98%). Balance-piston seal leakage does NOT change shaft power but does depress measured head and efficiency and raise the impeller inlet temperature (Eqs. 9-11, Figs. 4-5). The power litmus test ([W] p.62) - "the most important indicator of accuracy" power agrees + head/eta low -> GOOD DATA, the compressor has a real performance problem power disagrees + head/eta low -> BAD TEST DATA - check the flow measurement and gas sample first power agrees + head/eta ok -> PASS power disagrees + head/eta ok -> the POWER MEASUREMENT is the suspect, not the machine Plausibility ceilings ([W] p.62) - catch a bad reading before any tolerance does Polytropic head per closed impeller: 10,000-12,000 ft.lbf/lbm (as low as 9,000 in corrosive, yield-limited service). Polytropic efficiency: 75-78% for 2D vaneless-diffuser impellers, 80-85% for 3D vaned-diffuser impellers. A value above the ceiling means the measured compression ratio is too high, or the measured molecular weight is too low - not an exceptional machine. The vendor ledger - two different conversations A deviation between the vendor's stated value and this tab's independent recompute is a CALCULATION cross-check - the vendor's arithmetic disagrees with this tool's. Whether the MACHINE met its guarantee is the separate question the litmus test and the API 617 contract compliance card answer. Conflating the two loses exactly the distinction that matters first.

16. Site acceptance test (SAT)

Data reduction, uncertainty and acceptance for a site performance test. Source: Kurz, Davis, Kaiser, Brun & McBain, Site Performance Testing of Centrifugal Compressors and Gas Turbine Drivers, Turbomachinery Laboratory, Texas A&M, 2021 — cited as [SPT] — layered on ASME PTC 10, PTC 19.1 and ISO 5389. Equation numbers below are the paper's.

Data reduction — measured p, T, W in; head, efficiency and power out (7) ρ = p / (Z(p,T)·R·T) real gas, on the same EOS as the prediction (6) Q₁ = W / ρ₁ flow at the COMPRESSOR flange, not at the meter (8) H* = h(p₂, s = s₁) − h(p₁, T₁) isentropic head — fixed by the process data alone (9) H = h(p₂, T₂) − h(p₁, T₁) actual head — this is what sets the power (10) H_p = η_p · H polytropic head — needs η_p, which is iterative (11) η* = H*/H η_p = H_p/H (12) P_g = ρ₁ · Q₁ · H gas power (14) P = P_g / η_m brake power, η_m ≈ 0.98–0.99 (15) P = (1/η_m) · Σ P_g,section multi-section; internal leakage does not affect it Non-dimensional form — why a site test is compared this way (1) φ = Q₁ / (u_tip · D² · π/4) flow coefficient (2) ψ = 2H / u_tip² head coefficient, PER STAGE (3) Q₁/Q₂ held ⇒ same exit velocity triangle volume reduction ratio (4) Ma_u = u_tip / √(k·Z·R·T) machine Mach number (5) Re_u = D_tip · N · b_tip / ν machine Reynolds — TIP WIDTH b, not diameter Holding φ and ψ removes the need to test at exactly the predicted speed, and efficiency is already non-dimensional, so the three together pin the operating point. Two independent determinations of shaft power, then reconcile gas side P = W·(h₂ − h₁)/η_m the heat balance above driver side torque meter P = τ·ω the only DIRECT measurement (0.5–1.5 % of value) motor P = P_elec · η_motor · η_VFD · η_gear steam turbine P = W_steam · Δh gas turbine P = W_fuel · LHV · η_th, or — better — the compressor AS the load cell A gap wider than the combined uncertainty is an instrumentation finding, not a machine finding. [SPT] p.30: one case opened at 4.4 % and closed to near-identical once a leaking valve and a wrong flow-meter calibration coefficient were found. Uncertainty — ASME PTC 19.1, with finite differences PTC 10's polytropic work is iterative, so the Taylor-series partial derivatives are replaced by finite differences: perturb one measured variable by its systematic uncertainty, re-evaluate. (19) b_x = √( Σᵢ (Δᵢ)² ) Δᵢ = parameter(nominal) − parameter(perturbed) (20) U_x = √( b_x² + (t·s_x)² ) t = 2 for the 95 % form Random scatter sits far below systematic on a well-run test: [SPT] p.19 measured 2.3 / 2.5 / 2.6 % systematic on isentropic head, flow and power, against random std dev under 0.02 / 0.3 / 0.3 %. There is no single test uncertainty for a machine — a low-rise point can sit inside every PTC-10 limit and still be too uncertain to settle anything, because efficiency uncertainty scales with the ratio of the temperature error to the temperature rise. Steady state ([SPT] p.24) — stricter than PTC 10, and stated to be achievable in practice over a 10-minute interval, at least three data sets, all readings scanned at the same instant: speed constant within 5 rpm (full range) efficiency within ±0.5 POINTS of average head and actual flow within ±0.5 % of average Not a noise question: if the process is unsteady, storage effects break the conservation equations the whole reduction rests on. The verdict ([SPT] p.26) Only deviations OUTSIDE the test uncertainty are significant. If the uncertainty ellipse drawn around the test point still covers the prediction, the test does not contradict the prediction. Efficiency is checked on its own band rather than on the φ–ψ map, because ψ* is built from H*, which contains no discharge temperature — the ellipse is blind to an efficiency error. Reading a disagreement ([SPT] p.32) curve shifted horizontally, shape intact → flow measurement suspect some points on prediction, others not → gas composition varied during the test whole curve low, shape intact → machine condition, fouling When the acceptance conditions cannot be reached ([SPT] p.25) Prefer redefining the point over correcting it: pick the reachable point with the SAME φ and the same ψ*, have the OEM re-predict for it, and no Mach or Reynolds correction is applied at all. Correction is the fallback, and "any correction method will introduce a systematic error [which] will increase the larger the difference between the agreed upon and the as tested conditions".
