Assumptions and limitations

Purpose. This is an estimation and screening tool for preliminary sizing and for checking vendor proposals. It is not a substitute for a vendor selection, an API 617 rotordynamic analysis, or a rigorous process simulation.

Impeller eye geometry. The inlet relative Mach number needs an eye diameter and hub ratio that are really the vendor's to choose. A correlation of eye-to-tip ratio against flow coefficient with a fixed 0.30 hub ratio is used instead. This is the largest single simplification in the aerodynamic model.

Efficiency is correlated, not calculated. Polytropic efficiency comes from an inlet-flow correlation with a flow-coefficient derate. A real stage varies by several points with the vendor's diffuser and return-channel design.

Equal head split, constant diameter. Head is split equally between the impellers of a section and the tip diameter is held constant, so the flow coefficient falls stage by stage as the gas densifies. A wide φ spread across a section is the signal that intercooling or a second body is needed.

Frame rules are simplified. They encode common practice and the API 617 hydrogen partial-pressure rule for barrel casings. Vendor frame ranges differ substantially.

No phase equilibrium. Condensation at an intercooler is flagged from a Wilson K-value dew-point estimate only. No flash is performed and the composition is held constant through the machine — a real knockout drum would change it.

Equation of state accuracy. Peng-Robinson and SRK are reliable for hydrocarbons away from the critical point. Near the critical point, and for light gases such as hydrogen, helium and nitrogen well above their critical temperature at high pressure, all three models lose accuracy — SRK and Lee-Kesler usually do better than PR there. Compare models before trusting a number.

Rotordynamics. The bearing span, critical speed and cross-coupling figures are order-of-magnitude screening indicators only.
